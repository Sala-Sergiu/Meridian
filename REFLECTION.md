# Reflection

##What turned out to be harder than expected?
Technically, the application was not that hard, the hardest part was having to think through different situations and put myself in the shoes of people from other departements. Figuring out how to deliver a product that other people, with different needs from mine would actually use it successfully. The real challenge wasn't he code, but designing for someone that isn't me.

## Which decision would you make differently if you started over?
I would start on the front end in the beginning, or atleast work on it sooner. Building it earlier would have helpled me visualize what the application should look like in the end. It would give me something demonstrable from the start.

## What did you learn about yourself as a developer?
I learned that I work best when I decide the architecture before writting the first line of code, deciding what to keep, what to sacrifice, and what to leave for later. I can also let go of parts in the code without ego when it's necessary and I'm pressed for time.

One more thing, I have an instinct to perfect things, to add one more features. That's a good trait, but it depends on the context. Given my limited time here, I focused on that mattered the most for the application, and I realized that the discipline of not building is a muscle I'm still training.
